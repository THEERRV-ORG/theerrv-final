The instinct, once a tool frustrates you, is to build your own. Sometimes that's right. Often it's the most expensive way to solve a problem a ₹2,000/month subscription already solves.

Here's how we actually think about it — the same way we'd advise you if you called us tomorrow.

## Buy when the problem is common

If thousands of businesses have the exact problem you do, someone has already built a good tool for it, and they've spent years polishing it. Accounting, email, payroll, CRM, helpdesk, scheduling — these are solved. Buying means:

- You pay a fraction of what building costs.
- It works on day one, not month four.
- Someone else fixes the bugs and ships the improvements.

The moment you find yourself about to rebuild Gmail, Tally, or a basic CRM, stop. You will spend more and end up with less.

## Build when the software *is* your advantage

Custom software earns its cost when it does something a competitor can't copy from a pricing page — usually because it's shaped around **your** specific process, data, or customers. Build when:

- **Your workflow is the product.** The way you quote, dispatch, or fulfil is unusual and it's *why* customers choose you. Off-the-shelf forces you to work like everyone else.
- **The tools don't talk to each other.** You're paying five subscriptions and a person to copy data between them. The integration is the real product.
- **You've hit the ceiling.** A tool got you to here, but the "Enterprise" plan still can't do the one thing you need, and you're bending your business to fit its limits.
- **It's core, not admin.** Software that touches revenue or the customer directly is worth owning. Software that just keeps the lights on is usually worth renting.

> Rent your commodities. Own your edge.

## The costs each side hides

Neither choice is as cheap as it first looks.

**Buying hides:** per-seat pricing that balloons as you grow, data locked in someone else's format, and the slow tax of ten disconnected tools no one integrated.

**Building hides:** the maintenance that never ends, the "invisible 40%" of any build ([more on that here](/insights/what-custom-software-costs-in-india)), and the risk of reinventing something worse than what you could have bought.

## The move most people miss: do both

The best answer is rarely all-custom or all-bought. It's usually:

- ✅ **Buy** the commodity pieces (auth, payments, email, storage) instead of rebuilding them.
- ✅ **Build** the thin custom layer that connects them and encodes your actual workflow.
- ✅ **Integrate** so the bought tools feel like one system to your team.

That hybrid gets you the reliability of proven tools and the fit of custom software, at a fraction of a from-scratch build.
