People don't abandon forms because they don't care. They abandon because the form asked for more than the moment was worth. On a donation flow we built for a social service trust ([the full story here](/insights/website-for-social-service-trust-nallathae-nadakkum)), that insight shaped every decision — and the same thinking applies to any form you need people to finish.

## Start from the drop-off, not the fields

The usual way to build a form is to list everything you'd *like* to know and put a box next to each. That's backwards. Every field is a small ask, and every ask is a chance to lose someone. So we started from the opposite end: **what is the absolute minimum this transaction needs to happen?**

For a donation, the honest answer is almost nothing. The donor already decided to give before they touched the form. Our job was to not talk them out of it.

## Let the choice do the typing

Instead of a blank form, the visitor picks what they want to support — food, an ambulance, student support, last rites — as a single tap. That one choice then **auto-fills the details** behind it: which cause, which account, the suggested context. The donor never types what the system already knows.

> Every field you can pre-fill from a choice the user already made is a field you can delete.

This flips the effort. The person does the easy, motivating part — *choosing a cause* — and the software does the tedious part.

## Validate gently, in the moment

Nothing kills momentum like submitting a form and being thrown back with red errors. We validated inline, as people go — a phone number is checked as it's typed, not after — so mistakes are caught while they're easy to fix and the visitor is never punished for reaching the end.

## Meet people where they already are

The last decision was the most important. Instead of forcing donors through an unfamiliar payment page, on submit the flow does two things at once:

- ✅ Saves the request to the trust's dashboard, so nothing is lost.
- ✅ Opens **WhatsApp with the message already written** to the trust's number — the donor just taps send.

For this audience, WhatsApp *is* the internet. Meeting them there — rather than making them learn a new tool — removed the final wall between intention and action.

## The principles travel

None of this is specific to donations. Any form you need people to finish — a quote request, a signup, a booking — gets better the same way:

1. **Cut to the true minimum.** If a field isn't required to complete the action, remove it or ask later.
2. **Pre-fill from choices.** Make one decision populate the rest.
3. **Validate as they go.** Catch errors early and kindly.
4. **Finish where they already are.** Use the channel and payment method they already trust.
