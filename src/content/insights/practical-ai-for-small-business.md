Most AI advice aimed at small businesses is either breathless ("transform everything!") or dismissive ("it's a fad"). The truth is duller and more useful: AI is very good at a few specific, boring jobs, and pointless for many others. The winners aren't the businesses with an "AI strategy" — they're the ones who found the two or three tasks where it quietly saves hours.

## Where AI genuinely helps a small business
- **Answering the same questions, over and over.** A support assistant trained on your own FAQs, policies, and product docs handles the repetitive 80% so a human handles the 20% that actually needs judgement.
- **Turning documents into data.** Invoices, forms, PDFs, emails — pulling the useful fields out of messy documents is exactly what modern AI is good at, and it's pure time saved.
- **Search that understands plain language.** Letting staff or customers ask "how many orders shipped late last month?" and get an answer, instead of hunting through reports.
- **First drafts.** Product descriptions, email replies, summaries — not to publish blindly, but to start from something instead of a blank page.

The common thread: AI helps most where the work is **high-volume, low-stakes, and language-shaped.** A human still checks the important things.

## Where it's mostly hype (for now)
- **"AI will run your business."** It won't make your decisions or replace your judgement. It's a very capable assistant, not an owner.
- **Bolting a chatbot onto everything.** A chatbot that doesn't know your actual data just frustrates people more politely. The value is in the connection to *your* information, not the chat bubble.
- **Adopting AI to look modern.** If it doesn't remove a real cost or save real time, it's a science project, not an investment.

> AI earns its place the same way any tool does: it removes a cost you can measure. If you can't name the hours it saves, it's a demo, not a solution.

## How to adopt it without getting burned
1. **Start from a painful, repetitive task** — not from the technology. Name the hours it wastes today.
2. **Keep a human in the loop** where mistakes matter. AI drafts; a person approves.
3. **Mind your data.** Be deliberate about what information the AI can see, especially anything customer or finance related.
4. **Prove it small, then expand.** A two-week proof on one workflow tells you more than any vendor demo.
