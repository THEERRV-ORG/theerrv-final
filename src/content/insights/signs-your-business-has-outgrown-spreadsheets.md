Spreadsheets are the most successful business software ever made. They're free-ish, everyone knows them, and they'll happily run a company for years. The problem is they never tell you when to stop using them — the cost creeps up quietly, in lost hours and silent errors, until one day a broken formula takes down a process you didn't know depended on it.

Here are the signs it's happening to you.

## 1. The same data lives in five places
You copy numbers from one sheet to another, from email into a sheet, from the sheet into an invoice. Every copy is a chance to introduce an error, and none of the copies agree by Friday. When "which version is right?" becomes a regular question, the spreadsheet has become the problem it was meant to solve.

## 2. Only one person understands "the file"
There's a workbook with 14 tabs and colour-coded macros that one person built and only they can maintain. When they're on leave, the business slows down. That's not a spreadsheet any more — it's an undocumented application with a single point of failure.

## 3. You're afraid to open it
It's slow, it crashes, and everyone knows not to touch column F. Fear is a reliable signal: it means the tool has outgrown what it was designed for.

## 4. Simple questions take hours
"How many orders did we ship last month by region?" should take seconds. If it takes someone an afternoon of filtering and pivoting, your data isn't structured for the questions your business actually asks.

## 5. Two people can't work at once
Someone has the file open, so everyone else waits — or worse, works on their own copy that has to be merged later. Concurrent work is the moment most teams realise they need a real database behind the numbers.

## 6. There's no history and no rules
A number changed. Who changed it, when, and why? A spreadsheet rarely knows. And nothing stops someone typing text into a date field or a negative quantity — the validation that a proper system enforces automatically.

## 7. It can't do the next thing you need
You want a customer to submit a request themselves, or a dashboard the manager can check on their phone, or an automatic alert when stock runs low. Spreadsheets stop at the edge of the file. Your business doesn't.

> A spreadsheet is a great place to start and a dangerous place to scale.

## What replaces it — and what doesn't
The instinct is to jump straight to custom software. Sometimes that's right; often a good off-the-shelf tool covers it for a fraction of the cost — worth reading [our take on build vs. buy](/insights/build-vs-buy-custom-software) before you commit.

When the workflow genuinely is your own, the fix is usually a small, focused application with a real database: validated inputs, roles so each person sees what they should, a proper history of changes, and the reports your team actually asks for — the kind of [data and automation work](/services/data-automation) that turns a fragile file into a system you can rely on.

## The good news
You don't have to replace everything at once. The most successful moves off spreadsheets start with the *one* sheet causing the most pain, turn that into a small working tool, and grow from there.
