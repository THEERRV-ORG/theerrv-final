Every project starts with the same question, and almost no one answers it straight: **what does custom software actually cost?**

The unsatisfying-but-true answer is "it depends." The useful answer is knowing *what* it depends on — because once you understand the levers, you can read any quote and tell whether it's fair, padded, or too good to be true.

## Why two quotes for "the same app" differ 5x

You describe your idea to three teams and get back ₹3 lakh, ₹9 lakh, and ₹15 lakh. They're not quoting the same thing — they're quoting three different interpretations of what you said. The gap almost always comes down to a few things the cheap quote quietly left out:

- **Scope that was assumed, not written down.** "A booking app" can mean a single screen or a platform with payments, notifications, an admin panel, and refunds.
- **The invisible 40%.** Auth, roles, error handling, security, testing, deployment, and the admin side rarely make it into a demo but always make it into the bill.
- **Seniority.** A senior engineer costs more per hour and usually less per project, because they make fewer expensive mistakes.
- **Who owns it after launch.** A quote with no handover, documentation, or support is cheaper today and more expensive next year.

> The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest project. It's just the one that postponed the most decisions.

## The things that actually move the price

If you want to estimate before you ever talk to anyone, these are the real cost drivers, roughly in order of impact:

1. **Number of user roles and workflows.** One type of user doing one job is small. Customers *plus* staff *plus* an admin, each with different permissions, multiplies the work.
2. **Integrations.** Every external system you connect to — payments, WhatsApp, an ERP, a government API — is a mini-project of its own, with its own edge cases.
3. **Data and reporting.** Dashboards, exports, and "just one more report" are deceptively expensive because the data has to be modelled correctly first.
4. **Design fidelity.** A clean, standard interface is efficient. Pixel-custom, animated, brand-led design is a craft — and priced like one.
5. **Non-negotiables you can't see.** Security, reliability, and performance aren't features you demo; they're the reason the app still works at 10x the users.

## Rough shape of a budget

Without pretending to quote your project sight unseen, most engagements fall into recognisable bands:

- **A focused internal tool or MVP** — one clear workflow, a clean UI, a real database behind it. The smallest serious build.
- **A customer-facing product** — multiple roles, payments or integrations, an admin panel, proper testing. The most common "real" project.
- **A platform** — several connected modules, heavier data, higher reliability bar. Priced by module, not as one number.

The right move isn't to pick a band — it's to **scope a small first phase**, ship it, and let the working software inform the rest. Which brings us to the biggest cost lever of all.

## How to spend less without cutting corners

- ✅ **Start with a paid discovery.** A short, fixed-scope phase that produces a real spec and a real estimate costs a fraction of the build and prevents the expensive misunderstandings above.
- ✅ **Ship in stages.** Get the core workflow live, use it, then decide what's actually worth building next. You almost always cut your own wishlist once real usage speaks.
- ✅ **Say no to "while we're at it."** Scope creep, not hourly rates, is what blows budgets.
- ✅ **Buy where it makes sense.** Not everything should be custom. A good partner tells you when an off-the-shelf tool wins. *(More on that in [build vs. buy](/insights/build-vs-buy-custom-software).)*
